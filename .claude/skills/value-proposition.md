# Value Proposition Skill

> Create a clear, comprehensive value proposition using the 6-part structure with JTBD framing.

**Attribution:** Adapted from [phuryn/product-engineering](https://github.com/phuryn/product-engineering) value-proposition skill (MIT License, Copyright 2026 Pawel Huryn). See NOTICE.md.

## When to Use

- Creating value propositions for new products
- Refining positioning for a segment
- Comparing value against alternatives
- Aligning team on customer value

## The 6-Part Structure

### 1. Who
- Who is this value proposition for?
- What customer segment are we addressing?
- What are their characteristics and constraints?

### 2. Why (Problem)
- What is the customer's core problem or need?
- What's the Job to Be Done (JTBD)?
- What desired outcomes are they trying to achieve?

### 3. What Before
- What is the customer's current situation?
- What are they using today to solve this problem?
- What friction or pain exists in the current approach?

### 4. How (Solution)
- How does the product solve the problem?
- What specific features or capabilities deliver value?
- Why is this solution better than alternatives?

### 5. What After
- What is the improved outcome or future state?
- How does the customer's life/work change?
- What becomes possible that wasn't before?

### 6. Alternatives
- What other solutions could customers use?
- Why would they choose us instead?
- What's the switching cost or friction from alternatives?

## Process

1. Identify and profile the target customer segment
2. Define the core problem and JTBD
3. Describe the current state and friction points
4. Articulate how the product solves the problem
5. Envision the improved outcome
6. Compare against competitive alternatives
7. Create concise value prop statement (1-2 sentences)
8. Develop positioning statement for marketing use

## Example: Project Management Tool

| Part | Value |
|------|-------|
| **Who** | Product managers at mid-size SaaS companies (50-500 employees) |
| **Why** | They need visibility across multiple teams without creating overhead |
| **What Before** | Using spreadsheets + Slack + meetings; losing 5+ hours/week to status updates |
| **How** | Automatic progress tracking from tools teams already use; AI-generated summaries |
| **What After** | Real-time visibility in 5 minutes/day; teams focused on building, not reporting |
| **Alternatives** | Spreadsheets (manual), Jira (complex), Monday (expensive), asking in Slack (disruptive) |

**Value Statement:** "Product managers at growing SaaS companies get real-time visibility across all teams in 5 minutes a day, without adding overhead to their teams."

## Output Template

```markdown
# Value Proposition: [Product/Feature]

## Segment
[One-sentence description of target segment]

## The 6 Parts

### 1. Who
[Customer segment with characteristics]

### 2. Why (Problem)
[Core problem and JTBD]

### 3. What Before
[Current situation and pain points]

### 4. How (Solution)
[How we solve it]

### 5. What After
[Improved outcome]

### 6. Alternatives
| Alternative | Type | Why Us Instead |
|-------------|------|----------------|
| [name] | Competitor/Substitute/Do Nothing | [reason] |

## Value Statement
[1-2 sentence value proposition]

## Positioning Statement
For [target customer] who [statement of need], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], our product [key differentiator].

## Evidence
| Part | Evidence | Source | Confidence |
|------|----------|--------|------------|
| Pain point | [evidence] | [source] | High/Medium/Low |

## Assumptions to Test
| Assumption | How to Test |
|------------|-------------|
| [assumption] | [method] |
```

## Comparison with Strategyzer Canvas

This 6-part structure (from Paweł Huryn and Aatir Abdul Rauf) differs from Strategyzer's Value Proposition Canvas:

| Aspect | This Template | Strategyzer Canvas |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| Starting point | Customer (Who/Why) | Product often first |
| Segments | One per pass | May map multiple |
| Alternatives | Explicit section | No equivalent |
| Structure | Before → How → After | Jobs/Pains/Gains separated |
| Output | Ready statement | No reusable statement |

**Use Strategyzer for:** Detailed pains/gains decomposition of mature product's complex customer needs.

**Use this template for:** Clarity, speed, and actionable output.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] All six parts filled from evidence
- [ ] Statement names the alternative customer uses today
- [ ] Someone in segment would recognize their situation
- [ ] Assumptions marked, not presented as facts
- [ ] Clear differentiator vs. alternatives
- [ ] One segment only (different segments need different props)

## Integration

- **Value Curve:** Use to compare offerings on factors this segment values
- **Beachhead Segment:** Value prop follows segment selection
- **Proposal:** Value prop is part of proposal artifact
- **PRD:** Reference in problem statement and success criteria
