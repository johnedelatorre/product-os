# Beachhead Segment Skill

> Choose the first market segment to win before expanding to adjacent markets.

**Attribution:** Adapted from [phuryn/product-engineering](https://github.com/phuryn/product-engineering) beachhead-segment skill (MIT License, Copyright 2026 Pawel Huryn). See NOTICE.md.

## When to Use

- Choosing a first market for your product
- Targeting an initial customer segment
- Planning initial market entry strategy
- Deciding where to focus limited resources
- Validating GTM assumptions with early adopters

## Four Evaluation Criteria

### 1. Burning Pain Point

Assess whether the segment experiences an acute, unmet problem:
- Daily frustration with the status quo
- Significant productivity loss or cost impact
- Emotional urgency to find a solution
- Current workarounds that are expensive or fragile
- A problem getting worse over time

### 2. Willingness to Pay

Assess whether the segment has budget and motivation to pay:
- Documented budget allocation for this problem area
- Clear and compelling ROI (value > cost)
- Economic impact justifies solution cost
- Decision-maker with autonomy over budget
- No free or DIY alternatives that fully satisfy

### 3. Winnable Market Share

Assess whether you can realistically capture significant share:
- Segment large enough but not oversaturated
- Limited competition or easy differentiation
- Fragmented or complacent market players
- Clear competitive advantage
- Unique access or distribution advantage

### 4. Referral Potential

Assess whether customers will naturally refer the product:
- Professional communities within the segment
- Customers interact with adjacent segments
- High word-of-mouth culture in the industry
- Network effects within the segment
- Solving for one creates demand in adjacent segments

## Process

### Step 1: List Potential Segments

Brainstorm all possible target segments:
- Industry verticals (SaaS, healthcare, manufacturing)
- Company size (SMB, mid-market, enterprise)
- Job titles or roles
- Geographic regions
- Use cases or variations
- Customer maturity level

### Step 2: Research Pain Points

Validate burning pain in each segment:
- Customer interviews and discovery calls
- Problem validation through surveys
- Market research and analyst reports
- Competitor positioning and customer reviews

Quantify the problem's cost or impact. Identify current workarounds and their limitations.

### Step 3: Assess Willingness to Pay

Determine budget and economic viability:
- Budget for this problem category
- ROI calculation (value gained vs cost)
- Current spending on solutions or workarounds
- Budget decision-making process
- Typical deal size expectations
- Pricing sensitivity in the segment

### Step 4: Evaluate Winnability

Assess realistic market share potential:
- Total addressable market (TAM) size
- Competitive landscape and positioning
- Differentiation or unfair advantage
- Distribution access to this segment
- Time and resources required
- Market growth and momentum

### Step 5: Identify Referral Pathways

Map expansion opportunities:
- Adjacent segments influenced by reference segment
- Network effects within the segment
- Professional communities and associations
- Customer-to-customer recommendations
- Natural expansion path to adjacent markets

### Step 6: Select Beachhead

Choose primary segment across all four criteria:
- Highest combined score
- Most achievable for current resources
- Shortest path to PMF and revenue
- Best reference for adjacent expansion
- Most enthusiastic early customer cohort

## Scoring

**Every displayed score names its scale beside the number.**

Example scoring (1-10 scale):

| Segment | Pain (1-10) | WTP (1-10) | Winnable (1-10) | Referral (1-10) | Total |
|---------|-------------|------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------|
| SMB SaaS | 8 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 29 |
| Enterprise | 6 | 9 | 4 | 5 | 24 |
| Agencies | 7 | 5 | 8 | 7 | 27 |

**Mark scoring assumptions.** Don't invent evidence or universal quantified certainty.

| Score | Evidence Type | Confidence |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| 8 | 12 customer interviews | High |
| 6 | Team estimate, no validation | Low - needs testing |
| 7 | Competitor review analysis | Medium |

## Output

Produce beachhead analysis with:
- Top 3-5 recommended segments with scoring
- Primary beachhead segment recommendation
- Pain point validation and evidence
- Willingness to pay assessment
- Realistic market share projections
- Referral and expansion pathways
- 90-day customer acquisition plan
- Post-beachhead expansion roadmap

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] One segment chosen as primary
- [ ] Each score carries its source
- [ ] Assumptions marked, not presented as facts
- [ ] Evidence type noted for each criterion
- [ ] Owner can explain why it beats runner-up
- [ ] Expansion path to adjacent segments mapped

## Framework Reference

Based on Geoffrey Moore's beachhead market strategy in "Crossing the Chasm." Focus on the smallest winnable, referenceable market that validates PMF and enables expansion.
